## #70 — Story Loom, two sessions, 5 seats

### 70·1 + 73·4 — the board-loss guard fired on real losses, in two different teams · **EVIDENCE**

Story Loom: snapshot held 25, live board 0; recovered 25 cards intact. The Turborepo team, **the
second session running**: snapshot 35, live 0, restored 22 open cards.

The guard is `boardShadowWarning`, `team-convene.ts:118-132`. It snapshots the key's task count
**before** `bounty open` — _"after the open, a shadowing empty board is indistinguishable from a
genuinely empty one"_ (`:196-207`) — and warns without blocking.

**Why this is evidence rather than a thank-you: it bears directly on S13-N,** which says _delete
`boardShadowWarning` if spellbook's D1.3 lands — do not improve it._ The guard's own comment is the
reason:

> **IT CANNOT DISTINGUISH THE TWO WORLDS IT FIRES IN, AND THEY NEED OPPOSITE ACTIONS** — respawn-empty
> over an intact snapshot (recover, do not close) vs `close` already clobbered it (stop). _"From
> outside, both are 'snapshot > live'. `open` knows which; we cannot."_

**Both field reports are the recoverable world, and in both the recovery succeeded.** That is two
independent confirmations that the guard pays for itself in the world it actually fires in — so if
D1.3 lands and this gets deleted, **the recovery instruction must survive the deletion.** The value
is not the detection; it is the `Do NOT close the board` sentence, which is what saved 25 cards.

→ **BUILD:** the Turborepo team's sharper point — _"a warning that fires every session and requires a
manual out-of-band backup is a step away from being a procedure."_ Verified: the guard instructs no
backup. It says check `bounty sessions` and recover, and the team hand-rolled a backup before
touching anything because nothing told them to. **A guard that names a hazard and not its recovery
procedure is a procedure with a hole where the dangerous part goes.**

### 70·2 — `finalize-session` has no code-review touchpoint · **DESIGN** (the largest item in this triage)

**Verified absent as a beat.** The full close-out checklist (`finalize-session/SKILL.md:405-500`) runs
synthesis → seams → docs sweep → principle → retro → epitaph → seat-doc land → cross-seat land →
deferrals → board settle → human sign-off → `anthill down`. **No review item.**

What exists is all conditional advice, never a firing beat:

- `finalize-session/SKILL.md:337` — _"Commissioning a cold reviewer? GIVE IT THE TREE, NOT THE WIRE"_ —
  inside the retro section, phrased as _if_ you commission one.
- `join/SKILL.md:253` — _"Dispatching a subagent is available to you — it just never occurs to seats…
  an **option, not a mandate**"_, and `:258`'s cold-audit-before-you-post pattern, scoped to a _cut you
  own_, not the session's code.

**Their operating principle is already ours, which strengthens the case rather than weakening it.**
`field-notes.md:97` and `.anthill/principles.md:58` already say _"Dispatch an outside reviewer to FIND,
never to DESIGN — and reproduce before acting, either way."_ Their session reproduced it independently
at **4 of 4** — reviewer right about the finding, wrong about the remedy — plus a case of reviewers
_understating_ severity. **So the principle now has a third team behind it and nothing dispatches it.**

Why DESIGN and not BUILD: their shape carries four decisions that are not obvious, and the ritual is
already long enough that adding a beat is a real cost.

1. **Where it fires** — after green, before teardown. Their argument for this exact placement is the
   strongest part of the report: _"the seat that owns the contract is gone by the time its violation is
   found,"_ so review after teardown makes the lead apply every fix alone against four other seats'
   code.
2. **Scoped by dimension, not by file** — _"a reviewer handed one lane's diff only ever finds one lane's
   bugs."_
3. **Findings route to the owning seat via the roster; the lead rules disputes but absorbs no fixes.**
   ⚠ This one collides with **73·2** below — the roster cannot name the seams that matter, so routing
   by roster will mis-route exactly the findings that fall between seats.
4. **Reviewers are fresh subagents with no session context**, so they cannot inherit the team's blind
   spots. Consistent with `:337`'s existing reasoning.

**Precedent worth not tripping over:** session 7 dropped a "default code reviewer" item as
out-of-scope — _"the backlog file itself says the skill is `project-docs`, not anthill."_ That was
about shipping a reviewer _agent_. This is about a _beat in the ritual_ that dispatches whatever
reviewer the project has, which is the adapts-not-dictates split and a different question.

→ Take it to a convene. Decide 1–4 there, with 73·2 in the room at the same time.

### 70·3 — the retro must ask ONE literal, pre-committed question · **BUILD**, with a tension to resolve first

**Verified.** Step 4.5 (`SKILL.md:287-305`) asks three open questions — what went well / what didn't /
what would you change. Falsifiability is required only of **Q3 answers, after the fact** (`:307`:
_"Q3 answers are HYPOTHESES the next session can test, or they are not answers… A change you cannot
test is a preference wearing evidence's clothes"_). **Nothing anywhere asks the lead to pre-commit a
falsifiable question with a threshold before the session.** No pre-registration language exists in
`plugin/` at all; convene has no such step.

Their measurement is the good part. Their question was _"did you ever catch something by seeing
another seat's UNCOMMITTED work?"_ — **two seats answered yes with headline confidence and every
example turned out to be vine messages or landed commits**, neither of which is what was asked. The
reclassification, agreed by both, was the finding. _"A vaguer question would have banked two false
yeses."_

⚠ **The tension to resolve before this is built.** A pre-committed question announced to the seats at
convene is a **primed** question, and priming a behavioural question taints the answer — which is the
failure mode this repo already knows. The resolution is probably: **the lead pre-commits the question
and its threshold in writing at convene and does not announce it**, then asks it verbatim, first, at
the retro. That keeps the falsifiability and keeps the blindness. Whoever picks this up should make
that explicit rather than rediscovering it.

Note also that `.anthill/retro.md` already runs a **Verdicts** section scoring prior hypotheses
HELD/CONFIRMED/FALSIFIED/SPLIT/NOT REPRODUCED — **a convention of this repo's file that step 4.5 never
names.** If a pre-committed question ships, that section is where its verdict belongs, and it should
become shipped structure rather than local habit.

### 70·4 — `-m` and backticks · **COVERED**, and the second half is **EVIDENCE**

The recipe they landed on — write to a file, pass `--stdin` / `-F` — **is already shipped in four
places**: `comms/SKILL.md:212` (_"`--stdin` removes the whole class"_), `join/SKILL.md:337`,

**The second half is the valuable half, and it is not about backticks.** Their audit was _counting

**That is the same defect class we found in our own tree yesterday** — `team-join.test.ts:356-366`

sighting; it belongs in `principles.md` with all three scars attached.**

### 70·5 — a worktree does not isolate `git config` · **EVIDENCE**, nothing to attach it to

`git config` in a linked worktree writes to the **shared** `.git/config`; they disabled husky
repo-wide for ~2.5 minutes and landed three ungated commits.

**anthill has no worktree mode and no worktree guidance** — zero hits in `plugin/skills/` and

But the claim's _shape_ is already ours in two places: `finalize-session/SKILL.md:452` —
_"`refs/stash` is shared even across per-seat worktrees, where nothing else is"_ — and
`team-commit.ts:75-87`, which paid for it (_"every `anthill commit` in every worktree died with
ENOENT"_, in code whose comment named worktrees explicitly and was still wrong).

→ Park with `investigations/2026-07-27-shared-tree-failure-modes.md`. **If a worktree mode is ever
proposed, this is a precondition, not a footnote:** a linked worktree yields independent _checkouts_

`git status` for config**, so the sharing is invisible.

### 70·6 — a stale read on a shared tree has no as-of · **BUILD** (small)

Twice in one hour two seats, including the lead, made confident wrong claims about repo state because
the tree had moved — _"this route already exists"_ (a peer built it minutes earlier), _"your paths are
dirty"_ (they were committed). Method sound, instrument contaminated.

**Verified PARTIAL.** `.anthill/principles.md:155-163` already carries the sha half — _"A tree-grounded
claim travels with its sha, or it does not travel"_ and _"do not publish a count of a class without the
command that enumerated it AND the sha it ran against."_ **The mechanic is absent:** nothing anywhere
says to read the _committed_ version rather than the working file; `git show HEAD:` appears nowhere in
`plugin/`. The shipped as-of machinery (`comms send --as-of`) is about the wire, not the tree.

→ One amendment to an existing principle, not a new one: **a claim about what EXISTS is measured
against `HEAD` with a stated as-of, never against the working file — a claim with no as-of is
UNVERIFIED on a shared tree.** Cheap, and it closes a gap between a principle we already believe and
the command that implements it.

### 70·7 — seat attribution is a claim and needs a method · **BUILD**, and there is a mechanism

Twice a seat diagnosed a failing path correctly, **guessed** the owner, and landed an incorrect
attribution on a colleague; six messages went to correcting one wrong name. Their point: the standing
rule _"read the failing path before attributing the failure"_ tells you **which file** and says nothing
about **whose**.

**Verified ABSENT.** No guidance anywhere on determining who owns a failing path — `git blame`,
`git log -1 --format='%an'` and equivalents appear nowhere as guidance.

**And we have the scar already, in this repo:** `team-commit.test.ts:145-154` — _"their lead grepped it
for his own path, missed, and blamed peers for his own failure four times."_ The fix then was a parser
bug fix; **no attribution mechanic came out of it.**

**The concrete mechanism, and it is nearly free:** `anthill commit` already stamps `Anthill-Seat:` on
every commit, and its foreign-red diagnostic (`team-commit.ts:460-478`) already names the dirty paths
outside your commit — **paths, never owners**. The data to answer _"whose is this"_ is in the trailer.
Resolving path → last `Anthill-Seat:` and printing it beside the path turns a guess into a lookup at
exactly the moment the guess gets made.

⚠ Worth stating in the item: this is the class of claim where **being wrong lands on a person rather
than a build**, which is backwards from how carefully it was being treated.

## #73 — Turborepo monorepo, session 4, 4 seats

### 73·1 — "baseline at join / at close" is underspecified · **BUILD** (small, two sites)

**Verified PARTIAL.** The phrase ships at `docs-team/README.md:256` and `field-notes.md:130`, and both
say the same thing: _"Post **the gate's numbers** when you arrive and again when you leave."_ So it
does name what is measured — the gate — but **not against what tree state**, and nothing requires a
dirty-tree baseline to list its dirty paths or their owners. Their verify seat measured biome `EXIT=1`
that was a peer's file mid-edit, not a break; HEAD moved between two runs and neither run said so.

**Their remedy was also wrong, and they say so — which is the more useful half.** They adopted a
two-number form (HEAD via `git archive` + worktree), and `git archive` carries **only tracked files**:
a "HEAD baseline" read 599 files where the real tree reads 2579 and scans gitignored scratch.
Generalised: **a measurement technique carries its population with it, and one borrowed from another
question answers it silently.**

→ The correction is theirs and it is right: **the requirement is "measure a clean tree", not "measure
twice"**, and a dirty-tree baseline that does not list its dirty paths and owners is not attributable.
Two-site doc fix, plus the population lesson — which pairs with 70·6, since both are about an
instrument that does not report what it sampled.

### 73·2 — contract ownership and file ownership diverge · **DESIGN → investigation** (the structural one)

**Verified, and the mismatch is sharper than they could see from outside.**

- A seat's `scope` is a **free-text string**, not globs: `"plugin/scripts/anthill/ — the CLI commands,
config/coord/tmux, the migration engine, the comms wire, and their tests"`. **Nothing parses it.**
  Some scopes name a directory; `steward` and `scout` name a _function_ with no path at all.
- `seams.md` requires _"Each contract has ONE owning seat… Other seats **defer** to it."_ **So an
  unowned seam cannot be represented in the file that exists to record seams.**
- The nearest existing concept is unowned **documents**, not unowned code boundaries —
  `finalize-session/SKILL.md:195` and the whole rationale for step 3.75.

Their three instances are all real shapes: (a) a verify seat became a **land-time dependency** because a
ratchet fires when a consumer legitimately changes, making its author a required participant in every
land touching a consumer; (b) a seat correctly spent its last hours _outside its directory_ because
what it owns is the shape of the data and every consumer's contract with it; (c) **the load-bearing
seams were fetch↔render, helper↔consumer, instrument↔code — none of which a directory-drawn roster can
name, and the one nobody owned is the one that bit.**

**This is a model question, not a fix, and it has a dependency the reporters could not see:** it is the
same object as **70·2's routing decision** — _"findings route to the owning seat via the roster."_ If
the roster cannot name the seams that matter, roster-routing mis-routes precisely the findings that
fall between seats. **These two must be decided together or the review beat ships with a known hole.**

→ New investigation. It also touches `shared-tree-gate-tension` and the coordination-hardening arc's
phase 3, which the ROADMAP already flags as an unresolved collision to settle _at_ the re-triage.

### 73·3 — "split work by file, never by concern" · **DECLINE as worded** (half shipped, half contradicts)

Offered as a field-note candidate. It cannot ship in that form.

**anthill's actual model is concern-shaped and says so:** slices are _"one paragraph of scope per seat
(which owner owns what)"_ (`methodology.md:88`), and per-owner lanes carry the file-level HOW
(`:93`). **And `docs-team/README.md:172-181` explicitly expects two seats to write one file** and gives
the protocol for it — _"`seams.md` is where this recurs by design, since ownership there is
per-contract inside one file. So for a shared file: say on comms that you're taking it, and land your
edit promptly."_ A blanket "one seat per file" would contradict shipped guidance and break the
per-contract-inside-one-file case anthill depends on.

**What survives is their second sentence, and it is already ours:** _the split should be authored at
plan time by the seats who own the files._ `methodology.md:66-72` and `:93` already assign lane
authorship to the owning seat; `plan/SKILL.md:48` puts the skeleton before convene. **Their failure was
a build-time split authored by a seat who had never opened the peer's files** — i.e. shipped guidance
not followed, not shipped guidance missing.

The defensible narrow claim, worth a line somewhere: **the mid-write hazard is per file, so a
concern-split of one atomic unit violates it while sounding careful.** That is true and it is not the
same as "never split by concern".

→ Reply, decline the field note as worded, offer the narrow version.

`docs/backlog/2026-08-06-uncheckedagainst-reports-an-endpoint-not-a-delta.md` so whoever picks that up

**Praise attached, and it is load-bearing for S13-D:** _"`--as` is excellent and we would not give it

## #94 — Spellbook, 4 seats, 60-commit sprint (filed 2026-08-07)

### 94·main — generalise `gate` to a `procedures.land` slot · **DESIGN — accept the argument, re-site the implementation**

**Every one of their four claimed properties of `gate` is confirmed, and the intent is written into the
code in almost their words.** `config.ts:210-212`:

> `// No default: a wrong gate is worse than a named absence, because an agent`
> `// that runs someone else's gate command gets a green that means nothing.`

and the `ResolvedConfig` docblock at `:82-86` — _"anthill supplies the TRIGGER … the project supplies
the CONTENT. Hard-coding a gate here would be the convention-baked-into-a-default anti-pattern."_
**They reverse-engineered our own doctrine from the outside and named it correctly.** Absence is
announced at `team-join.ts:190-196`, and _more_ strongly than they claim — there are **two** refusal
branches, unset and non-composable, kept separate because _"they call for different actions by
different people."_

Their motivating case is sound and not hypothetical: 60 commits, 4 seats' trailers, **10 commit shas
cited by name inside living docs** because that project pins `file:line` claims to shas. Squashing
destroys 10 live references and 4 seats' attribution; for a single-author project squashing is right;
**no plugin can know which it is looking at.** That is the adapts-not-dictates split exactly.

Their two constraints are also right and should be kept: **prefer an executable `check` over a doc
anchor** (a renamed heading kills a pointer with no signal — the decay failure arriving via the
mechanism meant to prevent it), and **do not ship a general `procedures` map** — one demonstrated slot
beats an empty taxonomy.

**⚠ The one thing that does not survive verification is where they hook it, and it is the half that
decides the work.**

`gate` is resolved at **`anthill join`**, into a string the _seat_ later runs — `team-join.ts:574` is
the only read site, feeding `buildChecklist` → `buildLandCommand`. **anthill never executes the gate.**
Meanwhile finalize's sign-off beat is exactly where they say it is (`finalize-session/SKILL.md:490-494`,
_"Human sign-off before the code branch merges to `develop`"_) and it is **inert prose** — nothing at
that beat resolves, reads, or emits anything. `anthill down` is the only config-touching call in the
whole closing checklist.

**So "resolve the pointer and inline the content at the moment" needs a finalize-side resolver that
does not exist.** There is no counterpart to `buildChecklist`. That is the actual scope of this
proposal, and it is larger than adding a config key.

Three further design constraints the proposal should inherit, from how `gate` was built:

1. **One typed verdict feeding both the artifact and its absence-notice** (`GateDecision`), rendered as
   a **sibling, never concatenated** — a prior version inlined the notice into a string labelled _"run
   this EXACT command"_ and made the no-gate branch a shell syntax error carrying backticks.
2. **Keep unset ≠ empty ≠ malformed.** `gate` collapses empty→unset deliberately and keeps malformed
   separate.
3. ⚠ **Unknown config keys are silently ignored** — `resolveConfig` reads named fields and never
   enumerates `raw`, so a typo'd `procedure.land` would vanish with no error. **A slot whose absence is
   loudly announced but whose misspelling is silent has a hole in exactly the place this proposal cares
   about.** Worth fixing alongside, or the new slot inherits a defect the old one already has.

Also note: **archetypes carry no `gate`** (`layered-app.json`, `multi-surface.json` set channel/lead/
seats/grounding/launch and nothing else) — correct, and a `procedures` slot must follow the same rule.

→ Take to a convene with 70·2 and 73·2. It is the strongest single proposal in the queue and the
implementation is not where the reporter thinks it is.

### 94·2 — nothing records concurrent load · **BUILD** (real, and the reason is structural)

**CONFIRMED, and the mechanism is worth recording because it is not obvious.** The gate runs
**outside** the serialize lock — `buildLandCommand` composes `<gate> && anthill commit …`, so the gate
completes before `anthill commit` starts and therefore before `acquireLock` is ever called. **So two
seats can run two full suites simultaneously and both land with `waitedMs` absent and
`uncheckedAgainst: []`.** `waitedMs` measures commit-time queueing only; the lock's own note
(`lock.ts:15-22`) narrows it further — it cannot serialise against a peer's raw `git commit`.

`anthill status` carries no load, no running-process, no lock field. **The SOP compensates in prose
only** (`docs-team/README.md:250-258`, _"one gate run over the shared gate surface"_) — a convention
with no instrument behind it, which is their point exactly: _"an announcement is a record, not a
