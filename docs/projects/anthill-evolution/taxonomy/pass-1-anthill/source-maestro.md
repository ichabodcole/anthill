## Classes

Nine classes; every item assigned to exactly one; no unclassified remainder. Splits I resisted and merges I refused are noted at the end of this section.

### C1. Epitaph guards

Test: an item belongs here if it is a one-paragraph prediction of the successor's own characteristic failure, addressed to "you", and meant to be scored fired / did not fire.
Members (7): M1, M5, M9, M14, M179, M182, M184.
Prevention / usability: these are already the intervention for other classes; what they lack is a firing hook. Each epitaph should be paired with a one-line concrete tell ("when you type 'I verified', check the same sentence contains a command") and placed where the act happens (the send path, the finalize checklist) rather than at the top of a document read once at boot. The author's own evidence (M17, M24, M93) is that a warning read at boot does not fire at the moment of the act; only a check attached to the act does. A pre-send lint (does this ruling name an artifact? does it name what it overrides?) run by the comms tool would make three of the seven mechanical.
Specificity: any agent team. The seven are all about claims without artifacts (M1, M5, M14, M179), unwritten domain (M9), and framing vs facts (M182, M184); none depend on this project.
Recurrence: M179 restates M64; M5 is restated as M95; M14 as M71. Four of seven appear only once.

### C2. Guard bookkeeping and seat rotation

Test: an item belongs here if it is about the guards themselves — ranking, superseding, scoring whether one fired, or deciding who is allowed to report on them — rather than about the work.
Members (20): M3, M4, M8, M13, M17, M18, M23, M27, M28, M29, M30, M31, M32, M33, M35, M36, M113, M180, M183, M186.
Prevention / usability: this class exists because the ritual had no scorer. Specificity: any agent team that keeps written guards across sessions; the specific rotation argument is tied to this project's stigmergy thesis (M32) but the underlying measurement problem is general.

## Notes

- Readability, cold: hard. The document is written for a reader who already knows what the wire, the board, the vine, `#560`, R3/R19, Contract 6(g), H3/H4, steward/scout/forager/weaver/sentinel, `uncheckedAgainst`, and the whole-tree gate are. None are defined. I could follow the shape of almost every lesson (the author writes the general form in italics after each scar, which is what made classification possible) but the specific incidents are opaque without the session journals. The extracted-sections list at the top helped orient; the fact that sections are not in chronological order (session 11's reflection precedes session 1's; the lineage is at the bottom) did not.
- The material is about 60% failures, 15% structural verdicts, 10% practices that worked, 10% guard bookkeeping, 5% successor state. It is heavily weighted to the lead's own errors, by design.
- The author is aware of the recurrence problem and says so (M68, M73, M76, M181): several lessons are recorded as having been repeated within an hour of being written. That is the strongest signal in the file that prose lessons alone do not transmit, and it is the argument for the tool-hosted interventions above.
- The epitaph format (prediction addressed to "you", scored fired / not fired, superseded with reasons) is the most disciplined part of the document and the only part with a built-in falsification loop. The hard-won-lessons format has no such loop, which is presumably why it grew to ~70 bullets.
- The "right about where, wrong about what" seam finding and the "no reshape" verdict are each recorded in five or more sessions with the same answer. That looks like a questionnaire being answered faithfully rather than new information; the aggregate ("the roster has never needed reshaping; the seam contents have never been predicted correctly") would be a single line.
- Emphasis: a very large fraction of the text is bold or capitalised. After the first page it stops carrying signal.
- Two items are flagged by the author as most likely to be misread cold (M34, M118), and one (M115) is recorded as having been misread by its own author. All three are C9 successor-state items, which supports moving that class out of prose.
- Nothing in the file describes what went well from the product's point of view; every positive item is about process. I could not tell from this document alone what anthill does.
