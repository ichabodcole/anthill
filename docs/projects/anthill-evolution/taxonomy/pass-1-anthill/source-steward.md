## Classes

Eight classes, no unclassified items. Every item is assigned once. Tempted splits and merges are noted at the end of this section.

### A. Instrument reach — what the check could actually see

**Test:** an item belongs here if the core defect is that a tool, control, comparator, or verification produced a result that could not have distinguished the claim from its negation, and the lesson is about what the instrument looked at (its pattern, domain, fields, granularity, or independence).
**Members (37):** T2, T11, T13, T14, T15, T16, T30, T31, T35, T41, T42, T43, T53, T57, T59, T60, T65, T69, T71, T72, T73, T74, T75, T77, T80, T84, T87, T88, T99, T101, T108, T109, T111, T113, T114, T117, T121.
**Prevention / usability:** A mandatory _control line_ on any negative, zero, pass, or "confirmed" verdict before it leaves the seat: the message must carry (1) the exact command, (2) the enumerated domain the instrument touched (file list or count, not the verdict), and (3) a positive control run in the same command that could have come out differently for the same reason. The natural place to enforce it is the comms/send tool: refuse or warn on a message containing a verdict word ("zero", "none", "pass", "green", "confirmed") that lacks a `control:` field. Secondary practices, applied at the moment of reading rather than sending: read the raw envelope before any projection of it; `Object.keys` unioned across all members before auditing any; read an artifact once and derive every count from the same bytes. The author's own dispositional statement (T73: "ask what the instrument actually looked at") is the right shape, but this class shows it does not transfer by reading — it needs a structural hook at send time.
**Specificity:** General to any agent team that reports measurements. The concrete tools (`grep`, `prettier --check`, `bun test -t`, board JSON) are project colour; the shape (bad pattern, empty domain, truncated read, allow-list, proxy confirmation) is universal.
**Recurrence:** Seven pairs recorded twice — T31/T43, T59/T60, T14/T87, T15/T88, T11/T99, T9→T101, T2/T108 — plus T57 amending T53 and T107 amending T99. Roughly 23 items appear once. This is the most-recurring class by count and the author says so ("for the third session running").

### B. Inherited claims — numbers and verdicts that arrived looking settled

**Test:** an item belongs here if the error was accepting, relaying, or emitting a claim (a count, a roll-up, a retraction, a correction, an itemisation, a peer's account, a self-assessment) that had no command behind it, because it arrived already wearing credibility — one's own, a peer's, or the lead's.
**Members (16):** T1, T3, T4, T29, T48, T50, T54, T56, T61, T68, T70, T89, T105, T115, T116, T135.
**Prevention / usability:** A provenance column. Every verdict in a tally carries the command that produced it, and the aggregate is computed by a script over those rows rather than in the author's head; rows with no command are printed as a separate "residue" group, which is exactly the question (T105) that found the s13 error. The same rule extends to retractions, acceptances, and relayed claims: anything that changes a published verdict is a fresh row and needs its own command. Fires at publish time of any count or any message that reverses or repeats a prior claim; runs as the seat's own pre-publish step, or as a template the curate/lead step insists on before quoting a number.
**Specificity:** General. The mechanism (a claim inherits credibility from its packaging) applies to any agent that summarises or relays. The specific victims — retractions, "fourth surface", announced-vs-actual lands — are this team's, but a copying agent anywhere would hit the class.
**Recurrence:** Two pairs recorded twice (T29/T54, T105/T116); T1/T3/T4 are three facets of one incident recorded together. Twelve items appear once.

### C. Scope, population and definition — what set the claim ranged over

**Test:** an item belongs here if the measurement and its source were sound but the claim's population, axis, unit, category, or domain was unstated or wrong — including forgetting that the author is inside the set being counted.
**Members (14):** T51, T52, T62, T63, T76, T79, T81, T82, T91, T94, T95, T100, T102, T104.
**Prevention / usability:** A "population line" required on any claim containing a quantifier or a count: `over: <set>`, `derived by: <command>`, `unit: <bound|id|count>`, `includes me: yes/no`. The last field alone would have caught T51/T52/T104. For classifications, a required "MOOT / vacuous / unmeasurable" outcome so that a scheme with only done/not-done cannot be used. Fires before any team-ranging count or any definition that others will count with; runs as a checklist the seat fills and, where a term is being defined for the team, as a required field in the ruling itself.
**Specificity:** Mostly general (population, units, vacuous domains). The roster-vs-spawned instance and `--since` exclusivity are project-specific, but the class recurs in any codebase with overlapping sets.
**Recurrence:** Two pairs (T52/T104, T81/T91). Ten items appear once.

### D. Wording of the claim — right number, wrong sentence

**Test:** an item belongs here if the measurement was sound and the defect was in how the claim was worded, framed, hedged, or ordered — including jargon read in its plain sense.
**Members (9):** T19, T21, T22, T32, T38, T39, T44, T46, T85.
**Prevention / usability:** A send-time read of the sentence against the cell, with three concrete checks: the limiting condition sits in the claim's verb, not a footnote; any hedge ("does not license…") is in the same message as the number; any word that is a field name in the system (`emittedThrough`, `present`) is either used in the system's sense or replaced. The last one is lintable: grep outgoing messages for field names from the contracts file and flag them for a deliberate re-read. Runs as a pre-send step by the author; a reviewer cannot do it because only the author knows what the cell measured.
**Specificity:** The sentence-vs-cell gap is general to any agent that publishes measurements in prose. The jargon-collision item is project-specific (this team's contracts define ordinary English words).
**Recurrence:** No strict repeats; T46 re-uses T44's example to make a different point. All nine appear once.

### E. Directional rigour — how hard I checked depended on whom the claim favoured

**Test:** an item belongs here if the defect is that the author's rigour or eagerness to publish varied with the direction of the claim — indicting a peer or the lead, demoting or restoring the author's own work — rather than with the evidence.
**Members (6):** T33, T67, T97, T98, T122, T134.
**Prevention / usability:** A direction field on any message that cedes ground, accepts a reversal, or indicts anyone: "this favours ___; did I run the same instrument I would run for the opposite?" The author's own recognition rule (T97: "any message where you cede ground is an acceptance, and acceptances get audited") is the right form. It fires at send, run by the author; it could also be run by whoever curates the session — the pattern is visible from outside as "checked peer X hard at 20:00, took peer X on sight at 23:00".
**Specificity:** Seat-specific in its strong form (a checker's role frames it as outside the group, so indicting feels rigorous and yielding feels humble), but any reviewing agent will hit the weaker form.
**Recurrence:** One pair (T67/T122). The theme is also present in T48 and T11, which I classed elsewhere. Four items appear once.

### F. Moment-boundness — the world moved between the reading and its use

**Test:** an item belongs here if the defect came from an observation, message, or emitted artifact being tied to a moment that the world then moved past: two readings taken at different times on a live tree, a stale record that outlived the state it described, a broadcast or workaround that kept acting after its reason expired, or debris left by the author.
**Members (17):** T20, T34, T40, T45, T47, T49, T55, T64, T66, T83, T90, T93, T107, T112, T123, T132, T133.
**Prevention / usability:** Three mechanisms, all at the boundaries where something becomes durable. (1) Every landed record of a live state carries the watermark it was accurate as-of (a field, not prose), so the next reader can tell stale from wrong. (2) Any broadcast or workaround carries an owner and a retire condition; at finalize the owner is prompted to retire or re-affirm it. (3) A debris sweep at finalize — `uncheckedAgainst` already does this by accident (T112); make it a named step that reports unowned files. For readings: keep both snapshots and diff them instead of re-measuring; interleave A/B/A/B where possible; take pre/post from `git show`, never from the worktree.
**Specificity:** General to any multi-agent shared tree; the specific flags (`--as-of`, `--anyway`, `uncheckedAgainst`) are this project's answers to it.
**Recurrence:** Two pairs (T40/T83, T45/T47); T64 extends T132; T133 restates T78's incident. Thirteen appear once.

### G. Lesson form and recognition — how the doc itself fails to fire

**Test:** an item belongs here if it is about the form, transfer, replacement, or falsification of lessons and hypotheses — why a true instruction did not fire, how it should be worded, what would overturn it — rather than about any single incident.
**Members (21):** T5, T6, T7, T8, T9, T10, T12, T17, T18, T23, T24, T28, T37, T58, T78, T86, T92, T96, T106, T110, T136.
**Prevention / usability:** The author has already diagnosed this class (situational lessons do not fire; write the disposition). The missing intervention is structural: the curate step rewrites each incoming lesson into a fixed form — _trigger condition stated as a kind of result_ ("any zero, any pass, any list of who did what"), _the one question to ask_, _the falsifier_ — and drops the anecdote to an appendix. Then, instead of re-reading at join, the join step presents one novel instance (a different instrument, same shape) and asks the seat to classify it; recognition is drilled, not read. Falsifiers get a ledger so that "condition met" is a lookup, not a memory (T18, T24, T28 each had to reconstruct whether the predecessor's condition fired).
**Specificity:** General to any agent that carries written lessons across sessions. The epitaph ritual and its replacement notes are this project's convention, but the recognition failure is universal.
**Recurrence:** The core lesson ("re-read and it did not fire") is recorded at least five times in different words (T6/T7, T8/T9, T58, T78, T106, T110). Strict pairs: T28/T136, T23 quoted inside T18. About twelve items appear once.

### H. Seat role, value and attention — what this seat is for and where to aim it

**Test:** an item belongs here if it is a hypothesis, verdict, or rule about the seat's value, positioning, boundaries, or attention allocation, not about the correctness of a measurement.
**Members (16):** T25, T26, T27, T36, T103, T118, T119, T120, T124, T125, T126, T127, T128, T129, T130, T131.
**Prevention / usability:** These are not errors to prevent; they are a running experiment. What would make them usable: a hypothesis ledger separate from lessons, one row per hypothesis with its falsifier, the sessions it was scored in, and the verdict — the chain T126→T127→T128→T130 is already this in prose and would be one table. For attention: a counter — after N consecutive checks in one lane, the seat is prompted "what have you stopped looking at?" (T27/T129), which could be a self-imposed rule or a note the lead adds when assigning.
**Specificity:** Seat-specific. The anti-patterns (T118–T120) and the value hypotheses only make sense for a checking/verification role reporting to a lead.
**Recurrence:** One pair (T27/T129); T126–T128, T130 are one hypothesis re-scored four times. Ten items appear once.

### Tempted splits and merges

- I first had separate classes for _timing_ (two readings, different moments) and _afterlife_ (broadcasts, debris, stale committed records). I merged them into F because the author's own framing joins them ("`--as-of` refuses a stale message and nothing refuses a stale commit") and the intervention is the same: stamp the moment.
- I considered folding E into B (both are "accepted a claim without a command"). Kept separate because the mechanism differs: B is packaging (a claim looks settled), E is direction (a claim looks humble or rigorous). The author names E repeatedly as "the direction this seat over-reports in", so the material supports it.
- I considered folding H into G (both are about the seat rather than incidents). Kept separate: G is about how lessons transfer, H is about what the seat should do. Different consumer (curate vs. lead).
- T110 (heredoc) could sit in A as a tooling lesson; its headline is about why the warning did not fire, so G. T11/T99 could sit in E (explained away in the lead's favour); the rule stated is about readings, so A. T48 could sit in E; its stated rule is provenance, so B. T90 could sit in C; it is about publication having an effect, so F.

## Notes

- **Readability.** Hard for a no-context reader, and I had none. The material assumes fluency in the team's vocabulary — seat, wire, land, join, finalize, epitaph, board card, `--as-of`, `uncheckedAgainst`, `principles.md`, `seams.md`, Contract 6(a)/7, and peer names (weaver, scout, sentinel) — none of which is defined. About a third of the items I could only paraphrase by inference from surrounding sentences. The prose is heavily bolded and capitalised, which flattens emphasis; when everything is bold, nothing is.
- **Structure works against extraction.** The epitaph section is reverse-chronological with replacement notes interleaved between the quoted epitaphs, so a first read produces the impression that s13 replaced s12 replaced s11 before you learn what any of them said. Reading it bottom-up (s8 → s13) is easier. Several lessons carry inline amendments ("SUPERSEDED IN PART", "CORRECTED", "Beat 3.5") that change the lesson's meaning; a reader who stops at the bold headline gets the retracted version.
- **Self-referential and honest.** The document repeatedly records that its own prior content was wrong (T55, T86, T54, T57), which is unusual and useful, but it means the inventory contains items whose author no longer endorses them in their headline form (T53 is superseded by T57; T99 is qualified by T107; T21's falsifier was met per T18).
- **Two apparent tensions** I could not resolve from the material alone: T93 ("stamp it dirty") vs. T123 ("report it unmeasured") both concern gate runs on dirty trees and pull in opposite directions; T99 ("one reading is wrong") vs. T107 ("neither is wrong on a live tree") — the author resolves the second explicitly, not the first.
- **Density of the incident record.** Nearly every item carries a specific number (19.1%, 13m31s, 12 seconds, 20 of 20, 0 of 27), a command, and often a commit or message id. That makes the lessons falsifiable but also very long; most items would compress to one line of disposition plus one line of falsifier without loss, which is exactly what the author says in T10 and never does.
- **What is missing.** No item records something that went well without an accompanying failure; "gone well" appears only as controls that fired or peers' catches. There is no item about the seat's relationship to the human principal, though "my own principal" is mentioned once (T48).
