## 4. CONVERGENT FINDINGS

**(a) The largest class and its intervention.** Both merges find claims-outran-evidence the largest _failure_ class and converge on the same tool-hosted lint. A: "The lint shape is the strongest cross-source convergence in the material ... the tool, not the seat — six of seven sources say re-reading does not catch these and vigilance fails within the hour." B: "Converged proposal (5/5): a send-time / land-time guard, run by the comms or commit tool ... Every source names `--as-of` as the existing model ('the only mechanism that never needed anyone to remember it')." Proportions match: A-U1+U2+U3 = 471 of 1,392 (34%); B-U2+U3 = 657 of 2,006 (33%). (B's largest class by raw count is U5 product/design at 404, but that is contracts-file and builder-seat material — see §3.)

**(b) Recorded lessons do not fire for the recorder.** A: "Mechanism fires, disposition does not ... Scout: 'no epitaph ever fired on its holder at the needed moment.' Sentinel: 'a warning I have read and agree with does not become an action.' Maestro: lessons repeated within an hour of being written." B: "Written rules do not fire for their author (7/7) ... circe: 're-reading did not catch a single one — re-deriving from the tree did' ... daedalus: 'an epitaph does not fire in the session it is written in' ... retro R153: 'a doc you read is not a doc that fires'." Both draw the same conclusion: A "This is the cross-file finding that backs U8 and the tool-hosted shape of every U1/U2 intervention"; B "Every classifier draws the same conclusion in its intervention sections: the remedy is a tool at the moment of action, not more prose." Both also report _what did work_ was tooling: A "the shell operator in the land instructions", forager's isolation "it held"; B "`--as-of` ... 'not one refusal was noise'; `uncheckedAgainst`; `durationMs` on the commit envelope catching what a written rule did not."

**(c) Readability to a cold reader.** Seven of seven on both teams, for the same three causes. A: "undefined house vocabulary ... near-universal bold/caps so 'when everything is a headline, nothing is' ... non-chronological ordering (newest-first epitaph stacks scoring lines the reader has not met yet)." B: "a private vocabulary — seat, ward, cell, spell, gate, land, ratify ... used without definition in any file ... seams: 'so much is emphasised that emphasis stops discriminating' ... Ordering is not chronological (prospero, daedalus, retro newest-first)." Both add that the docs contradict their own stated purpose: A "Files contradict themselves and mostly say so"; B "Each document contradicts its own stated purpose (6/7)."

**(d) The retro's predictions and their follow-up rate.** Both find hypotheses under-scored and propose the same ledger. A: "capped at five per session (retro — S5's fifteen got four verdicts) ... R364 scored three ways in three sessions; R227/R232 absent from the next verdicts contrary to R233's own rule ... a row untouched for two (scout) or three (forager) sessions is flagged retire-or-run." B: "of 44 hypotheses, 17 got a real verdict, 8 only 'not tested', 12 never mentioned again; H22 'flagged most valuable' never run; ids H26–H32 reused across sessions; H34–H37 vanished." Follow-up rates are in the same band: A's S5 4/15 (27%); B's 17/44 (39%). Both also find the _remedies themselves_ were never run: A "the author names the mechanical fix and does not build it, or builds it and does not use it"; B "H17 ... never tested", "H20/H24 ... never tested", "H34 ... never tested", R77 "the clearest un-acted proposal across three sessions."

**(e) The contracts file.** Only B read one, so this is one-sided in evidence but convergent in shape. B: "The contracts file's content vs its stated purpose (raised in 5 files) ... 48 transferable rules + 30 incident records + 15 status markers + 7 provenance notes are 'not a data shape passed across a boundary' ... retro R78: 'the seams file is now doing two jobs (inter-seat interfaces vs how instruments lie); the second has outgrown it.'" A, without seeing its own seams file, _proposes_ it as a destination: U6 "route any lesson whose subject is a tool or ritual to the board or `seams.md` with an owner"; forager 5 "move each rule out of the seat doc into ... a `seams.md` clause". Both teams state the "point, never restate" rule and both report violating it: A "a one-home-per-claim lint"; B "at least three record having violated it in their own doc."

**(e′) The paper-cuts / friction log was built and then bypassed.** A residue P9: "no friction-log entry after 2026-07-05 while the retro records tooling defects through 2026-08-08 ... 'the tooling friction moved into the retro's structural, nobody's fault sections and lost the disposition row on the way.'" B: the paper-cuts method "was the right one and was not wired to anything that runs — 0 of 4 dispositions ever filled; 15 of 19 items never reached the file." Same proposed fix on both: wire it to finalize and refuse to close without a disposition.

**Further convergences.** Non-author review finds what the author cannot (A: retro RI's blank-context review "ran once in S4, found the most, never scheduled again"; B: seams S47/S271/S294 "the file's most actionable process finding", cassandra "not one was a false positive in my favour"). Recurrence is highest per item in the substrate/tool class (A-U5 "highest of any class relative to size"; B-U4 "highest recurrence relative to size"). Repeats are structural (A "a dozen distinct mechanisms told forty times"; B "roughly a fifth of the class is the same failure recorded again ... the class survives being written down"). The claim-vs-instrument boundary is contested for the same reason on both teams — the remedy fires in a different place (A: "harness vs send path"; B: "provenance on the _statement_ vs a control on the _instrument_") — and two classifiers per team refused the split (A: scout, retro; B: cassandra, thoth). Direction of error is seat-shaped and unenforced on both (A steward E; B "over-claiming and over-conceding are one failure"). Neither team has a prevention mechanism for composition questions.
