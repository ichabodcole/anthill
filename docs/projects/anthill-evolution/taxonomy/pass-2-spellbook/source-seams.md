## Classes

Eight classes, derived from the material. Every item is in exactly one class. Where an item carried two kinds (e.g. a wire clause with a maxim embedded, or an incident with a general rule attached), I classed it by its dominant kind and note the tension under "Notes".

Tempted-to-split/merge, and why not:

- Tempted to merge **F (rationale)** into **B (specifications)**, since the file's own template makes "Why it bites" a section of each contract. Kept separate: a rationale confers no guarantee, and the usability question for it is different (symptom lookup, not enforcement).
- Tempted to merge **H (provenance)** into **E (evidence)**. Kept separate: provenance says who decided and how independent they were; evidence says what was measured. They rot differently.
- Tempted to split **C (transferable rules)** into cross-seat engineering doctrine vs single-seat surface conventions (S131, S182, S196–S199, S209–S211, S214–S215). Did not, because that is an ownership axis, not a kind axis; the difference is recorded under C's "matches stated purpose" instead.
- Tempted to split **B** into wire/data shapes vs. governance clauses (repeal triggers S31/S38/S51/S267, the permission grant S43). Did not: the file's own preamble ("say where it ENDS") makes a contract's repeal bounds part of the contract.
- Tempted to make a "retained superseded text" class for S138. Folded into G (a supersession is a status).

### Class A — Rules for this document itself

- **Test:** an item belongs here if it prescribes how `seams.md` is written, maintained, or pointed at, rather than saying anything about the software.

## Notes

**Readability, as someone with no context.** Hard. The prose is dense, allusive, and written for insiders: seats are named after mythological figures (daedalus, circe, prospero, cassandra, thoth) with no key; "wards", "cells", "spells", "grimoire", "vine msgs", "drive-4", "F5", "b4s", "Slice 2", "R10", "Claim C", "P2 contact", "the C6 ruling" are all used without definition. Heavy bold-caps and ⛔/⚠ boxes signal importance but so much is emphasised that emphasis stops discriminating. The later contracts (16–21 and their amendments) are essays, not contracts. That said, the "Why it bites" paragraphs and the boxed corrections are genuinely readable and are where I learned what the system does.

**The file's kind drifts over time.** Contracts 1–15 (July–August) are mostly Class B — wire shapes, modes, layouts. From Contract 16 on (late August–September) the headline statements are Class C or D — lessons about instruments, populations, and vacuous checks, with the word "Contract" retained. The authors seem aware ("three instances in one sprint, which is what made it a contract rather than three findings", S269), but the admission test in the preamble was not revised to match.

**Contract 9 is a changelog.** Nine "rounds" of additive amendments, three explicit supersessions, one wholesale removal. Nothing consolidates them; a reader must apply them in order. This is where the file's S4 rule (say what it's true about, not how it's built) is most violated — table names, columns, helper-function names, and line numbers are all here.

**One un-reconciled contradiction.** S26 (Contract 2) says the `@source "./**/*.{ts,tsx}"` directive "must survive into any scaffold template"; S290 (Contract 21) says to use a bare `@source "./"` and not that glob because it misses `index.html`. Contract 2 was never amended.

**Embedded maxims.** Many Class B items carry a Class C maxim inside them (S185 "reject when a wrong value corrupts state; advise when it only degrades rendering"; S203's "house rule"; S165's "never hand-assemble a payload"; S201's rationale for 404-not-empty-list). I classed by dominant kind, but a reader mining the file for doctrine would miss these unless they read every wire clause.

**The strongest recurring finding is about discovery, not content.** Three separate times the file records that a stale or false sentence was found by a non-author (S47, S271, S294), and twice that the author of a rule re-committed its violation (S65, S252). The file does not draw the conclusion, but the material does: prose rules written by the people who then work under them do not self-enforce; independent re-reading and mechanised checks do.

**Numbers.** 319 items; 7 verbatim repeats; ~5 named recurrences of one lesson. Class sizes: A 8 · B 156 · C 48 · D 30 · E 41 · F 14 · G 15 · H 7.
