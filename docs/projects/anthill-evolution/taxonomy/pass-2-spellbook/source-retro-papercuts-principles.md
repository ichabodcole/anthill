**Readability, for someone with no context.**

Hard. The prose is dense, aphoristic, bold-heavy, and written in a private vocabulary (seat, lane, card, cell, ward, gate, land, ratify, calibrate, convict, costume, corpse, epitaph, canon, grimoire) that is never defined in these files; I inferred meanings from usage and am confident of most but not all (I read "cell" as a test case, "ward" as a guard test, "convict" as "make a check fail on a real defect"). Newest-first ordering means the file opens on the most cross-referenced entry and the reader meets verdicts before the hypotheses they judge; I had to read bottom-up to reconstruct the thread. The strong points: every retro follows the same four-question skeleton, every hypothesis has a falsifier column, every principle has a scar section and a subsumption check, and the writing distinguishes artifact from testimony with unusual discipline. The weak point is that the writing is better than the bookkeeping — the ids, counts, and cross-file amendments that would let a stranger (or a next session) follow a thread are exactly the parts that drift.
